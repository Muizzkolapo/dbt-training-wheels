{# Where a reader brings their SQL in, in the shape the design opens with: the promise, the box, and the four things this tool does with what goes in it. (new_design/dbt Training Wheels.dc.html) One card, not four boxes. The first build of this screen nested a card inside a card inside four fieldsets inside their inputs -- four concentric rounded rectangles, each with its own border, and a reader met a settings page rather than somewhere to paste a query. The card now holds one thing that matters, the query, with everything else on hairlines above and below it: where it is converting against, and what to press. Structure by rule rather than by box is what lets the SQL be the biggest thing on the page. The one screen in this directory with no conversion behind it. Every other screen explains a conversion's Decisions, and rule 1 in base.html exists so those explanations cannot drift from the report beside them. There are no Decisions here to explain -- and this screen still says something, because a reader who has not yet pasted anything is exactly the reader who needs to know what will happen to it. So the four lines come from `dbtw.core.intro`, not from here. That is the same rule as everywhere else rather than an exception to it: a sentence written in this file would either be unmarked, and fail the prose threshold, or marked, and fail the exact-match check against what `dbtw.core` produced. The project is required, and that `required` is not the gate three of three personas called theatre. That gate asked a reader to certify they had read something; this asks for the one input without which there is nothing to convert *against*. `test_no_screen_gates_the_write_action` holds the walk to the difference: the thing that must not be gated is the write. What the design shows here that this does not: tabs for "From warehouse" and "From git", both marked SOON. They are a roadmap, and a roadmap rendered as a disabled control is this screen promising something no code here does. The two sources that exist are the two that are offered.
