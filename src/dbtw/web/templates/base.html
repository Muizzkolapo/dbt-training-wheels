{# The frame every screen shares, in the shape the design gives it: an app bar across the top, a rail of steps down the left, and the screen itself on the right. (new_design/dbt Training Wheels.dc.html) Two rules hold for every template in this directory. 1. Every explanatory string is the engine's, and says so: it is rendered inside an element carrying `data-engine`, and a test checks each of those claims by exact match against what `dbtw.core` produced. Text outside such an element is this template's own, and no run of it may reach sentence length. 2. Every number is `len()` of the list beside it, or a position in one. The element showing a count carries `data-count=""` and each element of the list it counts carries `data-item=""`; an element showing a position carries `data-ordinal=""` and is held against its own place among those items. "4 new files" over a list of five is the mismatch that cost this page the most trust, and a step numbered 03 that is fourth in the rail is the same mistake about a different number. The rail is rendered only when there are screens to reach. While an answer is held whose question the current SQL no longer asks, there are none -- every screen refuses until it is dropped -- and a rail reading "0 screens" would say that as a defect rather than as a state. Each step carries a dot, and the dot is derived rather than decorative: a question nobody has answered wants an answer, an answered one is answered, and a screen with nothing to decide stands on its defaults. The legend beneath spells all three out in words, so the colour repeats the meaning instead of being the only thing carrying it -- and "Position in the flow never marks a step done" is the design's own note, which this rail keeps by deriving every dot from the conversion rather than from where the reader has got to. The glossary sits in the rail, beside the screen, rather than above or under it. Above was the first shape: it put a card of definitions on top of every page, so each screen opened on the glossary and its own title came second. Under it is the shape the report has and the walkthroughs showed does not work: four of five personas never reached a block that sat at the end. Beside it, the words are on screen at the same time as the sentences that use them, and the canvas opens on what the screen is about. On the entry screen there is no rail, so it renders in the canvas there. Each definition is folded, and the words are not. That is a correction made by looking at the rendered pages rather than at the HTML: `terms_in` gives a screen exactly the words it uses, which is the design, but the files screen uses nine of them and the done screen four -- so the block that was meant to save a reader from scrolling past twelve definitions was rendering a wall of nine, and putting "Write these files", the one control in the whole walk that touches their disk, below nine hundred pixels of glossary. A reader meets the words in the same place as before and one click from each meaning. `
` and not script, because `test_no_screen_gates_the_write_action` asserts these pages carry none. The typeface is the design's, fetched from Google Fonts with a system stack behind it.
